Space Travel Guide

Mercury

Mercury is the smallest planet and closest to the Sun. Its cratered, rocky surface faces extreme temperature swings and has almost no atmosphere.


Learn More About Mercury

Venus

Venus is the hottest planet due to a thick, toxic atmosphere that traps heat. It is similar in size to Earth and spins backward.


Learn More About Venus

Earth

Earth is our home planet and the only world known to support life. Liquid water covers most of its surface.


Learn More About Earth

Mars

Mars, known as the "Red Planet," is colored by iron-oxide dust. It is a cold, rocky world with giant volcanoes and dry riverbeds.


Learn More About Mars

Jupiter

Jupiter is the largest planet in our solar system. This gas giant is famous for its swirling bands of clouds and the Great Red Spot storm.


Learn More About Jupiter

Saturn

Saturn is a gas giant known for its bright, wide rings made of ice and rock. It is the second-largest planet and has dozens of moons.


Learn More About Saturn

Uranus

Uranus is a pale blue-green ice giant. It rotates on its side with a dramatic tilt.


Learn More About Uranus

Neptune

Neptune is the farthest planet from the Sun and a deep blue ice giant. It is famous for having the strongest winds in the solar system.


Learn More About Neptune

Honorable Mention: Pluto

Pluto, while not a planet by definition, is a dwarf planet in our solar system. It is smaller than Earth's Moon and features icy mountains, frozen plains, and a large heart-shaped glacier made of nitrogen ice.


Learn More About Pluto
